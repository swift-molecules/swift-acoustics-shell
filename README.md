# swift-acoustics-shell

Use this package when a sound comes from a curved shell, such as a lute's bowl, a bell or a cymbal, or from a body whose walls meet at an angle, such as a lute's top and bowl joined at the rim. Give `Acoustics.Shell` a midsurface as a mesh of quadrilaterals in space, a thickness and a material (and others for chosen faces), the direction the grain follows, the bars glued to it and what is held; `Acoustics.Shell.Modes` gives the lowest modes, each with its frequency, the motion of every vertex and its modal mass. Compute them once and keep the value. A mode's motion along a face's normal is what moves the air, which is how swift-acoustics-cavity takes a shell as a wall. Each face is a flat facet: the bending is swift-acoustics-plate's MITC4 element, the membrane a bilinear plane-stress quadrilateral, both on swift-galerkin's reference square, assembled by swift-galerkin into a sparse stiffness and mass of swift-sparse whose lowest eigenpairs swift-eigen finds. Materials are swift-acoustics-plate's, woods included.

## Laws

- **Geometry.** The mesh is a `Mesh<Point<3, Double>>` of swift-mesh, coordinates in metres; its faces must be quadrilaterals, and each is laid into the plane through its centre square to its normal there (x,ξ × x,η), so a warped face is taken flat. A face with no area, or one that turns over when laid flat, fails with `.face(index)`; so does a key of `thicknesses` or `materials` that names no face. A vertex no face uses is held. `normals` gives each vertex's unit normal, the area-weighted mean of its faces', pointing to where their corners turn counterclockwise.
- **Grain.** Each face's material has its first axis along `grain` laid into the face's plane, and its second across it in the plane. For a body of revolution, the axis laid into every face runs along the meridians, as a lute's ribs do.
- **Unknowns.** Each vertex has six: its motion along x, y and z and its turn about them. `Hold` holds any of them at chosen vertices (`.pinned` the motions, `.clamped` all six); a vertex that is not in the mesh fails with `.vertex(index)`.
- **Element.** In each facet's axes the bending is the plate's Reissner–Mindlin MITC4, with its shear correction, rotary inertia and tied shear strains; the membrane is the bilinear plane-stress quadrilateral at 2 × 2 Gauss points with consistent mass; and the turn about the normal, which neither resists, is tied to the membrane's own rotation ½(∂v/∂x − ∂u/∂y) by a penalty of 10⁻³ times the in-plane shear stiffness (Hughes and Brezzi 1989), so that a flat region has no free turn. A flat shell's bending modes are the plate's exactly.
- **Bars.** A `Bar` is a straight bar of rectangular section glued on one side, acting along the segment from `start` to `end` laid into each face it crosses: its centre line stands off the midsurface by half the shell's thickness and half its height, and it adds its stretching E A (ε + e κ)², its own bending E I κ², its twisting G J τ² and its shear κ G A γ² along that line, and its mass ρ A moving with its centre line. A bar along an edge counts half in each face. It keeps its true section wherever it lies on the mesh, so a bar needs no faces of its own. A bar with no length, no section or no side fails with `.bar(index)`.
- **Modes.** The `count` lowest, ascending, a repeated frequency once per mode. A shell held nowhere has its six rigid motions first, at zero to rounding. Each shape is every vertex's motion, scaled so that the longest is one and its largest component positive. `mass` is the modal mass at that scale. A shell turned in space rings as before; one scaled by s in every length, its thickness and bars included, rings at 1/s the frequency with s³ the mass.
- **Checked against** closed forms:
  - **swift-acoustics-plate:** a flat clamped spruce panel's first six modes, frequency, mass and shape, to 10⁻⁶.
  - **Love's equations** (Leissa 1973, Vibration of Shells, NASA SP-288; Soedel 2004, Vibrations of Shells and Plates, 3rd ed.): a steel cylinder of radius 0.1 m, length 0.2 m and thickness 1 mm, held by shear diaphragms, on 96 × 32 facets. Each mode is named by the waves its shape shows; those with n ≤ 6 round it are within 2%, and every mode's error falls more than threefold from the mesh twice as coarse. At n = 7 and 8, with 12–14 facets to a wave round, it is still 2–3% high.
  - **Lamb (1882)**, in the membrane limit (Kalnins 1964, J. Acoust. Soc. Am. 36, 74): a free steel sphere, a/h = 100, on 216 facets, its lower n = 2 and n = 3 frequencies, five and seven of each, within 2% of the sphere of equal area.
  - **A barred strip:** a strip 0.5 m × 20 mm × 2 mm with a 6 × 12 mm bar along the edge between its two rows of faces, simply supported, as a beam of their whole section about its common centroid, its first two modes within 1%.
